DEF CON

Attending My First DEF CON

This August, I had the chance to attend DEF CON 34 in Las Vegas: four days of talks, competitions, and conversations that genuinely changed how I think about cybersecurity. I dove into sessions on AI prompt injection, Microsoft device code phishing, and over-privileged cloud AI agents, competed in an AI pentesting trivia showdown in the AppSec Village, sat in on a sobering panel about the real-world policy failures behind the Change Healthcare ransomware attack, and picked up hiring advice from industry professionals that I'm still thinking about. I even stumbled through a classical cipher puzzle hunt embedded in conference art. DEF CON is loud, chaotic, and completely unlike anything else — and I left with a clearer sense of where I want to go in this field than I arrived with.

Competition

Integration Bee Challenge

On April 2nd, Kent State's math department hosted an Integration Bee — a bracket-style competition where teams raced through rounds of integration problems with fun puzzles thrown in between, all in the spirit of a slightly belated April Fools' Day celebration. My team made it to third place overall, but the highlight was walking away as the designated Puzzle Masters — the only team to get every single puzzle correct across all ten rounds. There is something genuinely energizing about being in a room full of people who think math is fun, where the atmosphere is equal parts competitive and joyful. It was one of those low-stakes, high-reward evenings that reminded me why I love math so much.

Art

Sketch Kent

On August 29th, I joined Sketch Kent — a local group of artists based in Kent — for a two-hour drawing session at the Haymaker Farmer's Market. We met at 9:30 in the morning and spent the next two hours wandering through the stalls, sketching and painting the scenes around us. I worked in charcoal, making quick figurative sketches of the market in motion — the busy exchanges between vendors and customers, the onlookers, the performers, the general organized chaos of a Saturday morning market. What struck me most was how differently each artist saw the same space. Same light, same crowd, same stalls — and every sketchbook told a completely different story. At the end of the session we gathered for what the group calls a "throwdown" — everyone lays their work on the ground, and each artist talks through what they made while the group listens, looks, and responds. It was warm and encouraging in the best way, the kind of low-pressure creative feedback that makes you want to keep making things. I left feeling genuinely refreshed, and I'm hoping to stay connected with this group for future meetups.

Research

Research Symposium

In January 2025, I began faculty-mentored research on privacy and security vulnerabilities in IoT and embedded systems, which led to my selection as a Summer Undergraduate Research Experience (SURE) fellow at Kent State. My research focused on the Meta Ray-Ban smart glasses — examining the privacy and security risks that emerge when a consumer device equipped with a camera and AI capabilities is worn on your face all day. I analyzed network traffic, studied data flows, and mapped the attack surfaces that most users never think about.

That research took me to two public stages: the Three Minute Thesis competition, which challenged me to distill months of complex work into a clear, compelling three-minute presentation for a non-specialist audience, and the Annual Undergraduate Research Symposium on April 13th, 2026, where I presented my findings alongside fellow student researchers from across the university. Walking visitors through my work was exciting, but what made the day memorable was the atmosphere — researchers from every discipline, all deeply curious about very different things, all in the same room. It was the kind of environment that makes you feel like learning and growing is exactly what you are supposed to be doing.

Creative summit

A2RU Emerging Creatives Summit

From March 5th to 8th, I attended the a2ru Emerging Creatives Student Summit at Michigan State University in East Lansing — an opportunity I received through Kent State's Design Innovation Fellows program.

The summit, themed "Rewilding: New Stories of Collective Wellbeing," brought together 72 students from 20 universities to collaborate across disciplines on creative projects exploring sustainability and interconnectedness. Our team interpreted the prompt "interconnected" through the theme of rewilding and created a stop motion piece — a medium that felt fitting, since stop motion is itself an exercise in interconnection.

Working with teammates from completely different academic backgrounds pushed me to think and communicate in ways I don't normally have to, and that friction was the most valuable part. Exploring Michigan State's stunning campus between sessions was an added bonus. I left with new collaborators, broader creative instincts, and a reminder that some of the best ideas come from people who don't think the way you do.
